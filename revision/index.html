Top of Memory

The boards, numbers and decision lines I keep at the top of my memory for system design interviews, plus the coding patterns: one daily set to recall, one reference to read, one coding deck.
Note

Revision pack, rebuilt from my own notes. Every card here is something I wrote while practicing: ten whiteboard boards, the numbers I keep in my head, and the decision lines I say before an interviewer asks. No lesson material from anyone else.

What this is

I kept re-reading long design write-ups the night before a practice session and remembering none of it the next morning. What survives a 60-minute whiteboard is small: the layout of the board, the five or six numbers that force the design, the API shape, the store choice, the failure line per component, and the sentence I say when I draw a box. This pack is only that, as 220 cards: a question with the answer folded away.

Three pages, used differently.

  • Daily: 41 cards I recall every day with the answer closed first. The opening script, the two board layouts, the six lines and the numbers to keep in my head, then the numbers block and the diagram order of every board. About fifteen minutes.
  • Reference: all 220 design cards by topic, in the order of the original pages. I read one or two sections a day in rotation and open only the cards I cannot say.
  • Coding: 116 cards for the other interview. The question side shows the cues of a problem or a pattern, the answer side names the pattern, the leap, the complexity and the trap. One section before the day’s two problems.

Marking a card hides nothing; it dims the card so the unknown ones stand out. The marks live in this browser only, and the Daily and Reference pages share them, because a card has one id wherever it appears.

How I use it

  1. Daily page first, phone or laptop, answers closed, say it or write it, then open.
  2. A card is known only after two clean recalls on different days: the first press marks it “once”, the second press on a later day marks it known. Known cards dim; the j key and the counters skip them.
  3. Every page, every day, but time only on the cards that are not dimmed; the first day is about an hour, from the second day the pass shrinks toward twenty minutes.
  4. One whole board on paper every morning in twelve minutes, plus two more boards as numbers and diagram order only; the whole-board cards carry a “drawn today” stamp so the boards I never drew stand out.
  5. One Coding section before the two problems of the day: say the pattern from the cues, then open.
  6. After every mock, each push I could not answer becomes a card the same evening, at the top of Daily.
  7. The day before the interview, reset the marks on every page and run a closed-book pass of everything; the evening before is light, and the morning of the interview is Daily only.

Four days to the interview

The compressed version. Every page every day, the slow treatment rotating through the sections, all ten boards on paper by day 4, and a closed-book exam on day 4.

Day Boards on paper Slow section Evening
Day 1 2 whole · 4 and 5 numbers and diagram API shapes · Data and stores design mock
Day 2 1 whole · 6 and 7 numbers and diagram Diagram patterns · the failure line design mock
Day 3 10 whole · 8 and 9 numbers and diagram Requirements and phrases · deep-dive material cold mock on a prompt from outside the pack
Day 4 3 whole · the weakest two again reset every mark, then the exam: closed-book pass of every page one last mock, then nothing new; in bed early
